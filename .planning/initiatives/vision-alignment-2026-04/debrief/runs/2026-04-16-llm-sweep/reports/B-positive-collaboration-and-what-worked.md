# Lane B Report

## Bottom Line

The strongest positive collaboration pattern was not generic agreement. Quality improved when collaboration created new control surfaces: user pushback triggered explicit correction and state-restatement, review gates were allowed to restructure the wave plan, orchestration shifted to the better-fit model with a real handoff, failed launch paths were reported rather than hidden, and the D4 audit slip was converted into a stronger Stage 3 protocol. The current debrief already captures the structural wins well, but it understates the interpersonal mechanisms that made them work. - User pushback often improved the shared state instead of just surfacing friction. Claude explicitly corrected wrong-path transcript search, rebuilt the signal index, and untangled Phase 4 status in response to challenges from the user. That made later work more trustworthy because the correction came with concrete restatement, not vague apology. Sources: `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/debrief/extracts/claude-chat-extract.md:20-31`; `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/debrief/extracts/cross-model-and-agent-usage-extract.md:20-28`.
- Review Gate 1 appears to be the highest-leverage collaboration moment in the whole initiative. Codex pushback changed the plan from Option `β` to `δ`, introduced the boundary memo, and split R5 into D1 plus D5; the process then treated that restructure as the right outcome rather than a plan violation. ## Patterns Worth Standardizing

- Keep `pushback -> direct correction -> concrete state restatement` as the default recovery pattern. It repeatedly improved clarity in the transcripts. Sources: `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/debrief/extracts/claude-chat-extract.md:20-31`; `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/debrief/extracts/cross-model-and-agent-usage-extract.md:20-28`.
- Keep review gates as real synchronization points. The best collaboration happened when the user remained the decision-maker and the next prompt waited for a filled Decision Record. Sources: `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/debrief/FINDINGS.md:16-28`; `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/handoffs/handoff-codex-primary-orchestrator.md:336-361`.
- Keep role-specialized collaboration explicit: user as bridge and final judge, Codex as primary executor/orchestrator, Claude as scheduled cross-model auditor, and agent/subagent paths when lifecycle control matters. Sources: `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/debrief/extracts/codex-chat-extract.md:24-30`; `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/handoffs/handoff-codex-primary-orchestrator.md:33-37`; `.planning/initiatives/vision-alignment-2026-04/handoffs/handoff-codex-primary-orchestrator.md:424-461`.

## Deterministic Implications

- Add positive signal classes for `correction_with_state_restatement`, `status_untangle_after_ambiguity`, `review_gate_restructure_adopted`, `role_transfer_with_rationale`, `execution_fallback_transparently_reported`, `draft_as_input_protocol`, and `agent_selected_for_lifecycle_control`.
- Weight a positive signal more heavily when it changed the artifact graph or protocol, not just the tone of the interaction.
- Preserve links from transcript moment -> durable artifact -> downstream consequence, because the best moments here improved later files and gates.
- Distinguish productive pushback from generic agreement. In this initiative, the highest-value collaboration moments often began with challenge.
