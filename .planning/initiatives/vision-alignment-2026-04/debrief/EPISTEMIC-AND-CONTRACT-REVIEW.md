# Epistemic And Contract Review

**Status:** Completed debrief review  
**Source spec:** `specs/spec-debrief-E-epistemic-quality-and-contract-adherence.md`

## Purpose

Evaluate whether the initiative's research and deliberations were trustworthy on their own terms, not just whether they happened to produce useful downstream outcomes.

This review answers:

- did the research meet the epistemic and methodological requirements in `../RESEARCH-PRINCIPLES.md`?
- did the deliberations remain reliable, traceable, and contract-shaped rather than drifting into label-level closure?
- did the work honor the assignments and cross-cutting constraints in `../BOUNDARY-CONTRACT-MEMO.md`?
- did execution meet the original goals of the orchestrator handoffs?
- where did the initiative deviate, and were those deviations justified?

## Context Budget

- Started with the spec's recommended core pack: `RESEARCH-PRINCIPLES.md`, `BOUNDARY-CONTRACT-MEMO.md`, `PLAN.md`, `SPEC-INDEX.md`, all initiative specs, all Wave 1 / 1.5 research files, and decision anchors `01-05`.
- Added only selective stretch reads after that: the two named handoffs, the two retained formal audit responses, the D2 underspec signal, and the Stage 3 synthesis outputs needed to verify whether deviations were corrected or merely papered over.
- Estimated budget outcome: slight stretch beyond the soft target, but still within the acceptable stretch. Justification: handoff-goal compliance, the D4 audit-protocol miss, and the D2 interface-completeness gap were not answerable from the core pack alone.

## Overall Assessment

The initiative was epistemically strong overall. The research corpus consistently followed the required methodological scaffold, resisted premature closure, marked scope expansions and gray areas explicitly, and produced deliberation-ready terrain maps rather than faux certainty. The deliberation corpus mostly preserved that discipline: D1, D3, D5, D4, and Stage 3 were contract- or ontology-shaped in the ways the memo and specs required, and the major reframings were marked rather than smuggled in.

The meaningful deviations were limited and legible. One was substantive-but-bounded: D2 closed the visualization contracts while leaving `AccessibleChartContract` and `AnchorRegistry` under-specified, which is an interface-completeness gap rather than a wrong architecture. The other was a real process failure: Claude bypassed the scheduled formal D4 audit protocol and accepted D4 through the advisory-review pattern first. That slip was later documented, audited post hoc, and not repeated in the Stage 3 application audit.

## Productive Reframings vs Process Failures

### Productive reframings

- R1/R4 refused backend-language and streaming-transport label traps, reframing both around stable boundary and protocol shape (`research/01-compute-backends.md:28-36`, `research/04-streaming-architectures.md:35-43`).
- R2 reframed "which chart library?" into renderer substrate, interaction contract, and anchor semantics (`research/02-visualization-at-scale.md:17-27`, `:334-346`).
- R3 reframed "learning views" into lesson graph plus artifact-binding architecture (`research/03-educational-content-architectures.md:17-26`, `:345-357`).
- R5 reframed "preset typing" into regulation identity, evolution, and execution handshake, then explicitly forced the D1/D5 split (`research/05-multi-regulation-typing.md:19-30`, `:229-255`; `handoffs/handoff-codex-primary-orchestrator.md:164-170`).
- R6 reframed performance from a viz-only question into coupled UI/render and job/compute envelopes (`research/06-performance-envelopes.md:25-31`, `:284-291`).

### Process failures or partial misses

- D2's missing `AccessibleChartContract` and `AnchorRegistry` definitions were a real contract-completeness miss, even though they did not block architecture closure (`.planning/knowledge/signals/f1-modeling/2026-04-11-d2-underspecified-interface-types.md:48-73`).
- Claude's D4 review bypassed the formal audit protocol and only later repaired the process through a post-hoc audit (`audit/2026-04-11-d4-acceptance-response.md:8-18`, `:221-245`).

### Unmarked drift assessment

I did **not** find a material pattern of unmarked drift in the core research/deliberation corpus. The important question changes were usually surfaced under `Reframing`, `Gray Areas Encountered`, or `Scope Expansion Notes`, then institutionalized in the memo, anchors, specs, or audits. The notable problems were not silent question substitution; they were interface completeness and one audit-protocol miss.

## Bottom Line

- Research quality: strong.
- Deliberation reliability and traceability: strong overall, with D2's interface completeness the main substantive blemish.
- Adherence to `RESEARCH-PRINCIPLES.md`: strong.
- Adherence to `BOUNDARY-CONTRACT-MEMO.md`: strong, including later governance carry-forward of cross-cutting constraints.
- Adherence to original handoff goals: materially successful, with one explicit D4 audit-protocol failure later corrected.
- Adherence vs incidental success: the initiative mostly succeeded by following its method, not by getting lucky. The exceptions were precisely the places where later artifacts had to add forcing mechanisms.
